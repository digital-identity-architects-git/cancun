Semantic Content Mapping to Target Pain Points on a Revenue-First Architecture

Most blog strategies chase traffic and hope revenue follows. I flip that. By mapping content to the real pain points your buyers are searching — and laying it onto a revenue-first architecture — every article earns its place by either capturing demand, building the topical authority that makes the rest rank, or moving a prospect closer to a sale. That's what makes it the right play for the long haul: the foundation compounds, the internal linking gets stronger every month, and the content keeps converting long after it's published instead of decaying into a traffic vanity metric.

1

The Koray Framework

Koray Tuğberk Gübür's framework treats a website as a semantic system — topical maps, source context, and entity relationships that prove to search engines you genuinely cover a subject. Done right it's one of the most powerful ways to build durable topical authority and rank for whole clusters at once instead of fighting for one keyword at a time.

The nerdy version

I follow the framework, but I run it with a revenue-first approach: I catch the BOFU and MOFU keywords prospects are actually using — as long as they'll look great next to the other blogs in the ecosystem — because those are people with problems already shopping for solutions. That gives a cleaner lead flow and the fastest route to revenue.

I also get the core pages done first, because we're going to be linking to them a lot. Bottom line: if you want someone who can look at your whole online footprint, place your content everywhere it needs to be, and say the right thing to both prospects and search engines, I'm a dreamy addition to the team.

2

Revenue-First Architecture

Before a single post is outlined, the site is structured around money. The pages and clusters that sit closest to a purchase get built and reinforced first, and everything else is organized to feed them. Traffic is a byproduct of a structure designed to convert — not the goal itself.

The nerdy version

It starts with buyer-persona building — getting specific about who's searching, what they're stuck on, and what would actually move them. From there I pick out the BOFU and MOFU keywords with real commercial intent and plan content and pipelines that always pick the next best move for an immediate revenue increase through organic visibility. Every cycle, the question is the same: what's the single highest-leverage piece we can publish next to grow revenue now?

3

The "Me Filter"

Every piece gets planned so it looks amazing in four places at once: on the blog roll, on the page itself, inside the internal link architecture, and to the SERPs. If it doesn't shine in all four, it doesn't ship as-is.

The nerdy version — problems the "Me Filter" prevents

With the filter in place you don't get orphan pages no one links to, thin posts that embarrass the brand on the blog roll, cannibalizing articles fighting each other in the SERPs, or content that ranks but reads like it was written for a crawler instead of a human.

The advantage: someone who plans every asset for SEO, AEO (answer-engine optimization), and overall brand image at the same time — so your footprint stays consistent, quotable, and credible everywhere it shows up, from Google to AI answers to the casual visitor scanning your blog.

4

Deserve the Top Spot

The most important part. Ranking #1 isn't something you trick your way into — you earn it by being the best answer. I do the competitive research, mine People Also Ask questions, and build a user experience on a page architecture that's contextual, engaging, and easily scannable — with calls to action placed exactly where a visitor might be frustrated, drawn in by a benefit, or in a pickle about something.

The nerdy version

Competitive research tells me what the current winners cover and, more usefully, what they miss. People Also Ask and related-search mining surface the exact follow-up questions real searchers have, so the page resolves the whole intent — not just the headline query. Then the architecture does the heavy lifting: contextual sections in the order a reader actually thinks, scannable structure for skimmers and snippets, and CTAs positioned at the precise moments of friction, desire, or confusion. That combination is what makes a page genuinely deserve the top spot — and keep it.

5

Multi-Site SEO & Content Management

Running one site well is table stakes. I manage SEO and editorial across multiple sites at once — keeping topical maps, internal linking, and brand voice coherent across a whole portfolio without letting any single property drift.

The nerdy version

Across a portfolio I standardize the parts that should be standardized — templates, briefs, tracking, and reporting — while tailoring topical maps and linking to each site's niche and buyer. That means shared systems and a single source of truth for performance, with the flexibility to prioritize whichever property has the biggest revenue upside this cycle.

6

Automated Production Pipelines

Strategy only matters if it ships. I build automated production pipelines that move work from keyword and brief to draft, review, and publish — so the team produces more, faster, and at a consistent quality bar.

The nerdy version

Briefs are templated from the topical map so writers start with intent, entities, and target links already defined. Repetitive steps — research gathering, formatting, internal-link suggestions, QA checklists, and publishing — get automated where it's safe to, with human judgment kept on the parts that actually need it. The result is a repeatable engine that turns strategy into published, optimized pages on a predictable cadence.
